Method

How the model works

A small firm designs and sells custom doubly curved aluminum facade and canopy panels, 4 × 10 ft, 3 mm 5052. Doubly curved surfaces cannot be made on a press brake or roll former: they need a die, a form, or a robot. Every order is a new geometry built as a batch of n identical panels. The economics run in model.py, the same Python file in the repository, executed in this page by Pyodide.

Q panels per year n panels per design D = Q / n designs per year w = w₀ · 0.5^(lead weeks / half-life) quote win rate L = D / w qualified leads needed hours(Q) = L·h_quote + D·h_design + Q·h_unit capacity(N) = N · h_year · (1 − m·(N−1)) m = coordination loss per coworker TC(Q, N) = N·(salary + overhead per head) + fixed + D·c_design + Q·c_unit + lead cost(L) feasible only if hours(Q) ≤ capacity(N) (no contractors) lead cost = c_L · [L + Lₛ/(γ+1) · (L/Lₛ)^(γ+1)] thin regional market Short run: N fixed. AC = TC/Q, MC = dTC/dQ. Long run: N*(Q) = fewest employees that can do the hours. LRAC(Q) = TC(Q, N*(Q)) / Q. MES = argmin LRAC. Smallest viable firm = smallest Q (and its N) with price ≥ LRAC.

Three routes

  • Tooled. A draw die per geometry, pressed at a host shop. Per-design cost: die, die engineering, tryout, expected rework after revisions, storage, inspection, press setup, financing until the customer pays.
  • Hand-formed. Employees form each panel over a CNC-milled buck. Almost no tooling, but about 60 skilled hours per panel. Forming cost shows up as headcount, not as a purchase.
  • Machina. The firm designs, sells and manages; Machina forms, scans and trims. Per-design cost is programming and first article; per-panel cost is robot time, finishing and freight.

Why average cost turns up

Three limits of a small custom firm: each employee has a fixed number of hours and there are no contractors, so growth comes in whole hires; each extra person adds coordination loss; and the regional market for bespoke facades is thin, so every extra lead costs more than the last. Slow lead times lower the win rate, so the same revenue takes more quotes.

Where the numbers come from

  • Machina capabilities: parts to 12 × 5 ft and 4 ft deep, sheet to ¼", ±1 mm or ±0.3%, 125 µin Ra, first parts typically within a week (Machina Labs). Machina publishes no pricing; its rates here are assumptions.
  • Air Force sustainment parts: six months faster and three times more affordable than before, per Warner Robins (Defense One).
  • Doubly curved facade panels at Dongdaemun Design Plaza: die casting about $7,000/m² and hydroforming about $3,000/m², versus $260/m² for a purpose-built multipoint stretch-forming process that took three years of R&D and 22,000 panels to justify (DDP study).
  • Stamping and draw-die costs and hidden die NRE (Jennison, DRA Metal).

Limits

Price is fixed, not competed down. There is one blended wage for designers and craftspeople, though skilled panel formers are scarce and would cost more. Machina's capacity and queue priority are not modeled. Installation, warranty and taxes are excluded.
